Budget the KV cache

Saved recomputation becomes growing per-request state.

KV caching is a time–space trade: retain projections of history so each decode step does not recompute them.

Without a cache, history is repeatedly projected

Correct but wasteful decode

At step t

Run the entire prefix (x_{1:t}) again to reconstruct every layer's keys and values.

Across T steps

Earlier positions are projected repeatedly even though their token identities do not change.

The model's hidden state at a position is layer-specific; we cache K and V for every layer, not one global token vector.

Resident bytes and bytes read per step are different

Two KV questions

Capacity

All live K/V tensors must coexist with weights, activations, graphs, and allocator/runtime state.

Traffic

Each decode query attends over historical K/V, so bytes consumed by attention grow with context.

A cache can fit yet still create a bandwidth bottleneck during long-context decode.

History length grows every decode step

Attention-side pressure
$$S_{kv}(t)=S_{prompt}+t,\qquad \mathrm{KV\ payload}(t)\propto B\,S_{kv}(t)$$
capacitygrows linearly in live tokens
per-step readalso lengthens for dense attention
This linear state growth is distinct from the quadratic prefill attention work commonly discussed for full prompts.

Reused prefixes change work and ownership

Not model semantics

Potential benefit

  • skip repeated prefix prefill
  • reduce TTFT for shared context
  • amortize prefix state across requests

System questions

  • who owns and evicts cached state?
  • what makes a prefix identical?
  • does reuse leak tenant information?
Do not conflate a model's per-request KV cache with a cross-request prefix cache.
Four tickets can be larger than forty

A request-count limit treats a 32-token completion and a 32k-token context as equals. The allocator cannot.

Failure scenario: admission counted active requests, not projected live tokens plus safety margin; a small burst of long contexts caused OOM.
